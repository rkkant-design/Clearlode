Headline finding

The parsing works — a messy 51-minute podcast + a press release became a clean structured record. But the scrub immediately caught a conflicting metric across two firsthand sources (resolution time "before" = 11 min vs 14 min). That single catch is the entire argument for keeping a human in the loop.

How we got it — the actual steps

Source-first, not keyword-first.

  1. Skipped open keyword search. A YouTube search for "customer support AI agent" returned Shorts, influencers and an ad — near-zero firsthand enterprise content. confirms the source-first rule
  2. Went to a known firsthand source — Sequoia's "Training Data" podcast — and opened the Klarna CEO episode. registry > search
  3. Pulled the transcript from YouTube directly — the "Show transcript" panel + official chapter markers gave timestamped text, free, no audio, no paid transcription. captions API path
  4. Fetched the first-party press release for the hard numbers, in Klarna's own words. first-party = in scope
  5. Extracted into the record schema, attaching a verbatim quote + source to every field. grounding rule
  6. Scrubbed: cross-checked numbers, caught the 11-vs-14-minute conflict, graded the evidence, flagged the walk-back as needing firsthand confirmation. the human step

Parsing verdict

Does it work? Yes — with the human step intact.

What worked
  • Structured extraction from a 51-min transcript + PR into the full schema.
  • Real verbatim quotes attached to problem, models, deployment, boundaries.
  • Free timestamped transcript straight from YouTube — no paid transcription.
  • First-party + firsthand sources cross-corroborated the headline numbers.
What still needs a human
  • Metric conflict (11 vs 14 min) — a machine would have picked one silently.
  • Exact second-level timestamps — chapters give sections; the stream confirms the second.
  • Metric hygiene — "handled" is containment, not resolution; must be labelled.
  • Firsthand test — the walk-back quote fails it today; held, not published.
The takeaway for the build

Auto-extraction gets you ~80% of a record cheaply. The last 20% — reconciling conflicts, pinning timestamps, enforcing the firsthand rule and metric hygiene — is the SCRUB, and it's exactly the part that makes FieldNote trustworthy. The pipeline should be built to hand a human a pre-filled draft, never to auto-publish.
