Core product thesis

Reduce the work between an AI signal and a business decision.

FieldNote helps a company decide which AI opportunities deserve action — using current developments, relevant use cases, credible evidence and explicit next steps. Its initial job is narrow on purpose.

What it is
  • A trusted decision layer between external AI developments and internal action
  • A repeatable decision record that changes or accelerates a real decision
  • Evidence-graded: fact, vendor claim and FieldNote analysis kept visibly separate
What it is not
  • A general AI news site or newsletter chasing volume and opens
  • A broad software directory or review marketplace
  • A replacement for technical, legal, security or implementation diligence

Reading time, page views and opens are supporting metrics — not the product outcome. The outcome is a decision made faster or better.

The problem

Companies aren't short on AI information. They're short on judgment.

Hundreds of models, agents and products ship every month. The unresolved task is deciding whether a development matters to a particular workflow, whether the evidence is credible, and what action is proportionate to the expected value and risk.

Every one of those gaps sits between an event and a decision. That gap is the product — and, as the market section shows, the evidence establishes the problem, not yet that FieldNote is the answer.

Evidence & editorial standard

Trust is a product requirement, not a nicety.

Every recommendation must let a reader trace the material evidence, understand its limits, and tell observation apart from judgment. Each claim carries a grade.

GradeDefinitionPermitted language
AIndependent production evidence — named org, method, measurable result"Observed in production" (stated scope)
BNamed customer case study or credible research with clear methodology"Reported" / "associated with"
CVendor documentation, release notes or sponsored case study"Vendor states" / "vendor-reported"
DDemo, announcement, anecdote or unverified secondary report"Early signal" / "not independently verified"

Minimum editorial controls

  • At least one primary source per development; direct links, publication and access dates
  • A named reviewer for high-impact recommendations
  • Visible separation of fact, vendor claim, FieldNote inference and recommendation
  • A correction history for material changes
  • Source-use policies respecting copyright, contract terms, database rights and robots restrictions

Problem boundaries

FieldNote improves use-case selection and external research. It cannot by itself repair poor data, weak integrations, missing governance or change management — and every recommendation must state when specialist technical, legal, privacy or security review is required.

Go-to-market

Do things that don't scale first — then automate what proved valuable.

The first offer is a hands-on (concierge) intelligence service with a lightweight portal. It creates real value, tests payment, and reveals which analysis is worth building into software.

Weeks 1–2 · Discovery
  • Interview 15–20 orgs (owners, revenue / yield / ad-ops leaders)
  • Document recent AI decisions, current research methods, time & spend
  • Identify the economic buyer, user, approval process and budget
  • Test observed behaviour and recent spending — not whether they "like" the concept
Weeks 3–6 · Concierge pilot
  • Secure ≥5 pilot commitments; ≥3 paid pilots
  • Deliver the offer manually via a simple portal / shared workspace
  • Track which signals are opened, saved, rejected and acted on
  • Record the reason for every decision and whether diligence or a pilot follows
Entry-offer pricing — research ranges, not list prices

Test ₹25,000–50,000 for a six-week India pilot and $500–1,000 internationally. Use structured pricing interviews and actual payment behaviour to revise them.

Target operating economics

≥70% gross margin at the business-plan level · ≤2 analyst hours per customer per month after first product release · falling review time without rising corrections · CAC payback matched to contract length and margin.

What could kill it

Failure modes to design against.

1

Going too broad

"Everything about AI for every company" is unbuildable as v1. The publisher wedge is the antidote.

2

Content as commodity

Summaries are cloned in a weekend. Defensibility is curation labor + the outcomes track record that only time produces.

3

Warm-access false positives

Friendly interviews feel like demand. Only paid conversion beyond your network counts.

4

Unscalable service

Concierge delivery can trap you as a research agency. The gate is a clear path to ≥70% margin after automation.

5

Name & trademark

FieldNote is the chosen name — but it collides with the well-known Field Notes notebook brand, and its domains and trademark have not been cleared. Confirm .com/.ai at a registrar and run an in-class (software) trademark search before committing spend.
