The build case

A cheap test worth running.

FieldNote turns the AI lessons buried in executives' public talks, podcasts and posts into a self-serve, evidence-graded library that tells an enterprise team what actually worked — in the operators' own words. It's the peer evidence buyers already trust, made searchable and decision-ready.

Read this as a skeptic — that's the point

This isn't a request to bet the company. It's a request to fund a ~6-week, one-person test, with almost no code and near-zero spend, that will prove or kill the idea against real users. The rest of this document earns that small "yes" — honestly, cons included.

The problem

Enterprises don't lack AI information. They lack trustworthy peer evidence.

Companies are past "should we use AI" and into "how — and what actually worked for someone like us." But two things are true at once:

  • The bets keep failing. The widely-cited figure is that ~95% of enterprise GenAI initiatives show no measurable P&L impact — mostly for organisational reasons, not technical ones.
  • Buyers have learned to distrust claims. 94% now fact-check what AI tells them, and they turn to peers precisely because vendor and AI claims can't be trusted; 74% lean on reviews and real deployments to decide.
  • But the peer evidence is scattered and locked. The honest lessons live in hundreds of conference talks, podcast interviews and posts — and in practitioners' heads. There is no trusted, decision-ready, firsthand place to find "what a company like mine actually did, and what they'd warn me about."

That gap — between an AI question and a confident, defensible decision — is the product.

What we're building

Firsthand deployment evidence, personalised to your role.

Fully self-serve: someone signs up, picks a minimal role-based profile ("Head of CX, retail"), and gets the deployment records that matter to them. Each record is one company's real AI deployment — in the operator's own public words (a talk, an interview, a podcast, the company's own post) — written up, graded A–D for evidence strength, with the lesson and, crucially, its boundaries and what went wrong.

The loop

Discover by role / workflow → Examine a sourced, graded record with the lesson and the gaps → Apply it to your decision. Over time, the record picks up outcomes, so it learns what actually held up.

Launch domain is customer support — one of the clearest near-term enterprise-AI ROI categories, where buyers are actively hunting for proof.

Why it's defensible

The UI is copyable. The corpus and the trust are not.

ZenML's free LLMOps database — now 1,182 curated case studies and heavily used — proves the category has real value. But it's engineering-implementation-focused, free, and neither role-personalised nor decision-oriented. That's the opening.

FieldNote's defensibility is a growing, curated library of firsthand operator lessons — evidence-graded, boundary-marked, role-mapped — plus, over time, a claims-vs-outcomes ledger (what the vendor promised vs what actually happened). An LLM clones the interface in a weekend; it cannot reproduce the verified corpus, the editorial trust, or the accumulated outcomes.

The honest version

On day one there is no moat — there's a thin library and a method. The moat is earned: curation labour + a track record that only accrues with time. Early on we win on focus, firsthand candour and trust, not defensibility. A skeptic should hold us to that, not to a fantasy of an instant moat.

Why now, and where we sit

The timing is real; the incumbents leave a gap.

Why now: the volume of public, firsthand AI-deployment talks and podcasts has exploded across 2024–26; cheap AI transcription makes "scrubbing" them viable; and enterprises have moved from experimentation to demanding proof.

The honest ledger

Pros and cons — both, plainly.

Why it can work
  • Validated demand — buyers actively distrust claims and seek peer evidence (94% fact-check AI).
  • Validated category — ZenML's 1,182-study library shows curated real-world evidence has pull.
  • Clear differentiation — firsthand lessons + grading + role + decision-readiness that no incumbent combines.
  • Cheap to start — sources are public; no customer onboarding; content is hand-producible.
  • Compounding asset — the corpus and outcomes ledger get more defensible with time.
  • Rights posture is favourable — short excerpts + attribution + transformation is textbook fair-use / research use.
Why it might not
  • Content is the bottleneck — the scrub is real labour, and the library is worthless empty (cold start).
  • Willingness-to-pay unproven — "why pay vs free podcasts / ChatGPT?" is the central question.
  • Thin moat early — defensibility is earned over months, not owned at launch.
  • Coverage gaps — some workflows (order-status) are genuinely thin in firsthand form.
  • Records decay — AI moves fast; freshness needs constant upkeep.
  • Founder-network gap — the team isn't inside enterprise customer-support.
  • Name not cleared — "FieldNote" collides with the Field Notes brand.

How to proceed

A de-risked path with a real off-ramp.

Don't build software first. Build evidence first — then let a hard gate decide.

0

Prove the record (weeks 1–3)

Scrub 20–25 firsthand records (21 candidates in hand): confirm the verbatim quote, log the timestamp, grade, mark the boundary. Stand up a no-code self-serve page (role picker + records). Measure cost-per-record.

~1 person · near-zero spend · no engineering
1

Put it in front of real buyers (weeks 3–6)

Get 10–15 customer-support leaders using it (warm outreach, the named speakers, CX communities). Watch what they actually do with it.

measure: repeat use · evidence used in a real decision · willingness to pay
✓

The gate — go / no-go

Continue only if, say, ≥5 come back, ≥2 say it changed a decision, and ≥2 would pay. Miss it and we stop — having spent weeks, not quarters.

an explicit, pre-agreed kill criterion
2

Only then, build (month 2+)

The thin self-serve product — record store, search, role feed — plus semi-automated scrubbing. Funded by evidence, not faith.

build against proof
We're not starting from zero

Most of Phase 0 is already done.

  • 70+ candidate deployments researched and graded; 21 firsthand records ready to scrub.
  • The editorial method — evidence grades A–D, boundary discipline, metric-normalisation, a rights policy.
  • Three worked records (Klarna, Chime, Lightspeed) as the quality standard, plus a taxonomy and a production tracker.
  • Standing firsthand sources identified (podcasts, conference channels) that refill the pipeline indefinitely.
  • Honest sizing — a realistic bottom-up Year-3 range of ~$0.3–3M ARR, built from unit economics, not a borrowed TAM.
The ask

Fund a six-week test, not a company.

Green-light one person for ~6 weeks to run Phase 0–1 against the explicit go/no-go gate. It is a small, reversible decision. If it works, we've found a compounding, defensible asset early and cheap. If it doesn't, we've lost weeks — and learned something real about the market either way.

The question isn't "is this a billion-dollar company?" It's "is it worth six weeks to find out?" — and the honest answer is yes.
