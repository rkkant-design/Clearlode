Launch definition

One sentence everyone agrees on.

Definition of "launched"

FieldNote is live when a customer-support leader can self-serve: create a role profile, browse and filter a library of 40–60 human-verified, evidence-graded firsthand CS deployment records, open any record through its TL;DR → decision card → evidence, and ask a grounded, cited chatbot — all on a stable, publicly reachable deployment.

This is a quality-over-volume launch. The corpus is intentionally small and fully verified. Coverage and automation grow after launch; trust does not wait.

The boundary

In scope / out of scope.

In scope for 31 Oct

Product features

  • Role-based profile (role · industry · size · workflows) — self-serve, no onboarding call
  • Filtered library — facets: company · category · models · ROI / evidence grade + search
  • Tiered record view — TL;DR → decision card → evidence (quotes + timestamps)
  • Grounded RAG chatbot — answers from verified corpus only, cited, server-side, rate-capped
  • Evidence grade A–D + "verified" badge on every record
  • "Why this matters to you" fit note per record

  • 40–60 firsthand CS records, human-SCRUBBED, graded
  • Workflows: tier-1 deflection · order-status · agent-assist

Quality bar — non-negotiable

What makes a record fit to ship.

A record counts toward the 40–60 only if it clears every one of these. These do not bend under deadline pressure.

  • Firsthand — a named person from a named company, in their own public words (YouTube).
  • Customer-support — maps to one of the three launch workflows.
  • Human-SCRUBBED — a person confirmed each quote and timestamp against the source.
  • Grounded — every claim (number, model, architecture) has a verbatim quote + timestamp; absent = not_disclosed, shown as such.
  • Graded — carries an evidence grade A–D with metric hygiene (deflection ≠ resolution ≠ containment ≠ handle-rate).
  • Boundary-marked — caveats / what the operator would warn a peer about are visible.
Launch acceptance criteria

We have launched when all are true.

  • ≥40 verified records live, each meeting the quality bar
  • A new user can create a role profile and see a relevant feed
  • Filters work across company, category, models, ROI/grade + search
  • Every record opens through all three tiers with working source links + timestamps
  • The chatbot answers from the corpus with citations and refuses out-of-corpus questions
  • Off-scope NEO content is excluded from the FieldNote library
  • Deployed to a stable public URL; auth works; smoke tests pass
After launch — how we judge it

The success gate.

Launch is the start of the measurement, not the finish line. Over the weeks after go-live we watch three signals to decide whether to invest further:

  • Repeat use — do the same CS leaders come back?
  • Decision impact — did a record change or inform a real decision?
  • Willingness to pay — would they pay for it?

Exact thresholds are set by the Product Lead before launch. Missing the gate is a signal to stop or pivot cheaply — the whole point of a small, honest launch.

Assumptions & locked decisions

What this scope depends on.

Assumptions (if any breaks, the date is at risk)

  • 1–2 developers (one who knows the NEO codebase) + 1 curator, from day one.
  • AWS + Bedrock access, budget, and NEO repo/data/key handoff completed in week 1.
  • Firsthand CS source material exists in enough volume to reach 40–60 (order-status is the thin spot).

Decisions locked this cycle

  1. Adapt NEO, migrate to AWS now — not a fresh build, not NEO's current stack long-term.
  2. Strict firsthand customer-support scope — the moat over volume.
  3. ~40–60 verified records at launch — explicitly not 1,000.
  4. Chatbot = consumption model B, grounded + server-side (user spends no tokens).
  5. Stack-fallback accepted as date insurance.
Change control

After sign-off, any addition to "in scope" must name what it displaces — a deferred item stays deferred, the date stays fixed, or the record count drops. Scope grows only by explicit trade, never by drift.
