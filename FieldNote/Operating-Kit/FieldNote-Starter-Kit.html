Starter kit · Customer Support

Build one credible record before you build anything else.

This is Stage 0. It gives you the four things every FieldNote record depends on — a template, a worked example, an editorial method, and a rights policy — so you can hand-produce your first customer-support deployment records and prove the core is real, at near-zero cost.

How to use this

Copy the template for each deployment you research. Fill it from public sources, follow the editorial checklist, and time every record. Your goal this week: 3–5 records that a customer-support transformation lead would call "decision-ready" — and a real number for how long one takes.

3 · Editorial method

How every record is made — and maintained.

1

Capture the claim

Retain the exact excerpt, source date, speaker and role, and a timestamp/link. Quote — don't paraphrase — the load-bearing sentence.

2

Describe the evidence

Label it: company-reported, vendor, independent, or estimate. Note corroboration (single source / corroborated / independently confirmed / pending). Assign a grade.

3

Review the context

Check definitions, baselines and measurement periods. If a baseline is missing, the outcome is directional at best — say so. Keep every gap visible.

4

Update the record

Track corrections, changed outcomes and new evidence over time. A record is a living thing with a version and a corrections log.

Evidence grades

AIndependent production evidence — named org, method, measurable result with baseline.
BNamed case study or credible research with clear methodology.
CCompany/vendor account or single-source interview — plausible, not independently confirmed.
DAnnouncement, demo or anecdote — not verified.

Pre-publish checklist (every record)

  • Every material claim has a dated source, a type and a grade
  • Baselines are present, or explicitly marked "not disclosed"
  • Company vs vendor vs independent is labelled, not blurred
  • At least one boundary / condition / gap is stated
  • Conflicting evidence is noted where it exists
  • Only short excerpts used — linked and attributed, nothing reproduced wholesale
  • A reviewer (not the author) signed off, with a date
  • Time-to-produce recorded

5 · Sourcing & rights policy

Firsthand and public — the only sources.

Records come only from the named practitioner's own words, publicly available. The test: "Would the named person recognise this as their own public statement?" If yes, it's in. If it's someone else's account of them, it's out.

In — firsthand & public

  • Video — YouTube talks, conference keynotes, recorded interviews
  • Audio — podcast interviews
  • X — the practitioner's own posts
  • The company's own public writing — its engineering blog, newsroom, press release, an exec's earnings remarks

Out — someone else's account

  • Vendor case studies — the vendor's words about a customer
  • Journalist / press articles — the reporter's words
  • Analyst reports, and anything gated or licensed

The discipline

  • Excerpt, link, timestamp. Transcribe for your own analysis, quote a short excerpt, cite the link and — for video/audio — the timestamp. Never republish full transcripts or host the media.
  • Respect the platform. Honour each platform's terms of service; don't redistribute downloaded video or audio.
  • Quote ≠ analysis. Keep the speaker's words and FieldNote's interpretation visibly separate.
  • Right of reply. Named people can respond to material claims; corrections are logged, not silently edited.
  • No fabrication, ever. Missing data — and unconfirmed timestamps — are marked as such. One invented number or fake timestamp destroys the moat.
When in doubt

If it isn't the named person's own public words — or you can't point to the exact video, audio or post it came from — don't publish the claim.

The gate & your week

What "decision-ready" means — and where to start.

A record is decision-ready when a customer-support transformation lead could act on it without chasing the original sources: every material claim is sourced, dated and graded; baselines are present or marked missing; at least one boundary is stated; conflicts are noted; and a reviewer signed off.

Do this week

1. Duplicate the template. 2. Produce 3–5 records from firsthand public sources only — a named operator's talk, interview, podcast or their company's own post — scrubbing the exact quote and logging the timestamp. 3. Run each through the checklist and time it. 4. Ask two people in a CS role: "could you make a decision from this?" If yes, and a record takes a sane amount of effort, the core is real.
