Interview kit · the moat layer

Get practitioners to tell you what the case studies hide.

Vendor case studies give you breadth with no baselines. Interviews give you the truth — the real starting point, the cost, what broke, and the lesson with its conditions. This kit is how you find those people, get the call, and pull out a record-grade story. Every interviewee is also a design-partner candidate.

1 · Where to find them

Target the people who actually shipped it.

You want operators, not vendors. Job titles that signal a real deployment owner:

  • Titles: Head/VP/Director of Customer Support · CX Operations · Support Operations · Conversation Design · "AI/Automation Lead, Support" · Customer Experience Transformation
  • LinkedIn search strings: ("customer support" OR CX) AND (AI OR chatbot OR "agent assist" OR deflection) filtered to Director+ · or search a known vendor's name + "we deployed / we rolled out"
  • Communities: Support Driven (Slack + community), CX Accelerator, RevGenius/CX channels, relevant subreddits, and vendor user-community forums
  • Conference speakers: anyone who gave a support-AI talk already wants to share — reach out with "loved your talk, can I go deeper?"
  • Warm intros: ask your network "who do you know running AI in a support org?" — a referral triples your reply rate
  • Podcast guests: guests on CX/support-AI podcasts are pre-qualified and reachable
Aim

Book 3–5 interviews to start. Send ~20 requests to get there. Each good interview yields one high-grade record and a design-partner conversation.

2 · Outreach templates

Lead with the value exchange, not a pitch.

You're not selling — you're offering to trade their 25 minutes for the synthesized findings across every deployment you study. That's a genuinely good deal for an operator.

Cold LinkedIn / emailHi [Name] — I'm building FieldNote, a research library of real enterprise AI deployments in customer support, so teams can learn from what actually worked instead of vendor hype. You led [Company]'s support-AI rollout — I'd love 25 minutes to learn what really happened: the baseline you started from, what surprised you, and what you'd tell a peer. In return you'd get the synthesized findings across every deployment we study, and early access as a design partner. Worth a short call in the next couple of weeks?
Warm intro (forwarded by a mutual)Thanks [Introducer]! [Name], great to meet you. I'm researching how support teams are really deploying AI — order-status, deflection, agent-assist — and turning it into a decision-grade library for other teams. Your work at [Company] is exactly the kind of story I'm trying to get right. 25 minutes, your schedule? Happy to share everything I learn back with you.
Follow-up (one, after ~5 days)Hi [Name] — circling back in case this slipped by. Even 15 minutes would be hugely useful, and you'd get the full cross-company findings in return. If now's bad, is there someone on your team who owned the rollout day-to-day?

Note the last line — it converts a "no" into a referral to the person who actually knows the details.

3 · The interview guide

Twenty-five minutes, six questions, one record.

Open by restating the deal and consent (below). Then work these six — the whole point is to extract what a case study leaves out: the baseline, the cost, and the failure.

1. "Walk me through the deployment — what workflow, what stage is it at now, and when did it go live?"
→ Deployment · Workflow · Stage · Technology
2. "What actually changed — and how did you measure it? What was it before you deployed?"
Pull hard for the baseline. "Faster" is worthless without the before-number.
→ Reported outcome (+ baseline)
3. "Roughly what did it take to build and run — team, time, cost? Has it paid back yet?"
You won't always get a number; get the shape of the cost.
→ Cost & ROI
4. "What went wrong, or surprised you? What did you have to fix after launch?"
This is the gold no case study prints. Let them talk; don't fill silence.
→ Challenge
5. "If a peer were about to do this, what would you tell them to do, to avoid, and when to pause? Where does your experience not apply?"
The lesson AND its boundary — both, always.
→ Lesson (+ conditions)
6. "Anything you can point me to — a talk, a post, metrics you're able to share? And who else should I be talking to?"
Evidence to attach + your next two interviews.
→ Evidence · Referrals
Consent — say this up front

"I'll write this up as a short record. I can attribute it to you and [Company], or keep it anonymous — 'a mid-market retailer' — your call. And I'll send you the write-up to check before anything's published." This gets you candor and keeps you clean on rights.

4 · Turn it into a record

Within an hour of the call.

  • Right after: drop the six answers into the record template while it's fresh; mark every number's baseline (or "not disclosed")
  • Grade it: a single candid interview is usually Grade C (company-reported, single-source) — it jumps to B if a public talk/post corroborates it
  • Cluster: if you already have a secondary-source record for this company, merge — corroboration raises the grade
  • Boundaries: write down what they told you does not transfer
  • The design-partner ask: "This is exactly what I'm building for teams like yours — would you want early access and to shape it?" → that's Stage 2 beginning
The double win

Every interview produces a high-grade record and a warm design-partner. Your sources and your first users are the same people — which is why Layer 2 is worth the effort Layer 1 can't replace.
