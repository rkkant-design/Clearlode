Methodology · how we grade & recommend

On what basis FieldNote recommends.

A recommendation is only worth trusting if you can see how it was reached. Every card is scored on four axes, then mapped to an action by a fixed rule. Nothing rests on opinion or a vendor's press release.

1 · Evidence grade — how well substantiated

AIndependent production evidence — named organisation, method, measurable result. Language: "observed in production".
BNamed customer case study or credible research with clear methodology. Language: "reported / associated with".
CVendor documentation, release notes or sponsored study. Language: "vendor states".
DDemo, announcement or unverified secondary report. Language: "early signal, not verified".

2 · Verified outcome — what actually happened

The vendor's claim is checked against outcomes FieldNote has tracked across real pilots. The gap between claim and verified reality is itself the intelligence — and the proprietary asset a summary can't reproduce.

3 · Fit to your business

Does it address a stated priority, and are the conditions where it works present at your company? A strong average outcome with poor fit is still the wrong bet.

4 · Complexity & risk

Implementation effort, downside exposure and reversibility.

The decision rule

AdoptStrong evidence (A/B) + consistent positive verified outcome + good fit + low downside.
PilotPositive but variable outcome, or strong fit on a high-effort response — prove it in your context against a control before rollout.
InvestigateReal potential on vendor-grade (C) evidence — get methodology and references, then a small controlled test.
WatchWeak evidence or a negative track record — do not act; the downside outweighs the upside.

Note: pilot counts and verified figures in this sample dossier are illustrative. In production they are drawn from FieldNote's tracked-outcomes ledger, which accrues with every customer pilot.
